How Schedule Loss works

Methodology

Schedule Loss treats the NBA schedule as a set of game-level conditions that can make one team's situation more demanding than its opponent's. Rather than assuming every back-to-back, road trip, or travel event has the same effect in isolation, the model compares the number of active schedule disadvantages on both sides of each matchup. The difference becomes that game's schedule advantage or disadvantage, and those game-level differences are accumulated across the season to describe each team's overall schedule environment.

1. Data sources and season scope

The schedule database is built from the official 2026–27 NBA regular-season schedule. Each game stores the date, local and Eastern start time, teams, venue, venue location, and derived schedule context for both teams.

The current release contains 80 assigned games per team. The two remaining games per team are determined by NBA Cup results, so congestion and travel measures around those open dates should be treated as provisional until those matchups are added.

Arena analysis uses four completed post-pandemic seasons of TeamRankings against-the-spread data, covering 2022–23 through 2025–26.

2. Basic comparison rule

For every matchup, Schedule Loss counts the active disadvantage factors for each team. The team carrying more factors is classified as having the schedule disadvantage; the opponent receives the corresponding relative advantage.

Game Factor Margin = Team Disadvantage Factors − Opponent Disadvantage Factors

A margin of +2 means the team has a 2-factor disadvantage. A margin of −2 means the team has a 2-factor advantage. If both teams have the same number of factors, the conditions cancel and the game is treated as neutral.

More factors = disadvantageOpponent has more = advantage

3. Game-level schedule factors

FactorDefinition used in the current model
Back-to-BackThe team's previous scheduled game was on the immediately preceding calendar day.
3 in 4The team plays at least three games within the four-calendar-day window ending on the current game date.
5 in 7The team plays at least five games within the seven-calendar-day window ending on the current game date.
Less RestThe team enters the game with fewer rest days than its opponent. Rest differences are counted by game, not by the number of extra rest days.
1-Game HomestandAn actual home-arena game where both the immediately previous and immediately next scheduled games are away from the team's home arena.
Home After 5+ RoadThe first game back in the team's home arena immediately after at least five consecutive games played away from its home arena.
2+ Hour Time-Zone ChangeThe absolute change in venue UTC offset from the previous game is at least two hours on the relevant game dates.
Awkward StartA disadvantage only when the game tips before 1:00 PM or at or after 8:00 PM in the venue's local time and the team's home arena is at least two hours of time-zone offset away from the game venue on that date. A noon game in New York therefore does not penalize the Knicks, but can penalize a Mountain or Pacific time-zone visitor.
Arena DisadvantageThe home team's historical Home ATS% exceeds the visiting team's historical Road ATS% by at least 8.0 percentage points. The visitor receives one disadvantage factor, producing a one-factor relative arena advantage for the home team.

Each qualifying condition contributes one factor. Overlapping congestion conditions can therefore stack: for example, a game can simultaneously be a back-to-back, a 3-in-4, and a 5-in-7.

4. Advantage / Disadvantage profile

The red and green boxes on each team page classify games by the net factor difference between the two teams.

  • 1-Factor Advantage: opponent has one more disadvantage factor.
  • 2-Factor Advantage: opponent has two more disadvantage factors.
  • 3-Factor Advantage: opponent has three more disadvantage factors.
  • 4+ Factor Advantage: opponent has four or more additional factors.
  • The disadvantage row uses the same logic in the opposite direction.

Games with equal factor counts are neutral and do not appear in either row.

5. Cumulative Advantage

Cumulative Advantage is the main season-level summary. It preserves the severity of multi-factor games rather than simply counting favorable and unfavorable games.

Cumulative Advantage = Total Advantage Factor Margins − Total Disadvantage Factor Margins

A 3-factor advantage contributes +3; a 2-factor disadvantage contributes −2; a neutral game contributes 0. A positive season total indicates a more favorable schedule environment under the current model, while a negative total indicates a greater cumulative schedule burden.

7. Arena Advantage

The arena component is designed to capture home environments where historical performance against the betting market has been materially stronger than the visiting team's road performance.

Arena Matchup Differential = Home Team Home ATS% − Visiting Team Road ATS%

If the differential is +8.0 percentage points or greater, the home team receives a relative Arena Advantage and the visitor receives one Arena Disadvantage factor. The factor changes the net matchup by one; it is not counted twice.

Team Arena Profile, shown on the Arena Advantage tab, is a different descriptive statistic: a team's own Home ATS% minus its own Road ATS%.

8. Travel and location

Travel from the previous game is measured as great-circle distance between venues using their latitude and longitude. This provides a consistent approximation of geographic travel burden, but it is not intended to reproduce the exact flight path or door-to-door itinerary.

Time-zone changes use venue UTC offsets on the relevant game dates so daylight-saving changes are reflected where applicable.

9. Hours since previous tip

Game profiles report the elapsed hours from the scheduled start of a team's previous game to the scheduled start of the current game. This uses each venue's local time zone and therefore captures short turnarounds more precisely than calendar-day rest alone.

Interpretation and limitations

Schedule Loss is a schedule-context model, not a game prediction model. The current version gives each active factor equal weight and is intended to identify unusually favorable or difficult scheduling situations. It does not yet estimate how many points a factor is worth or the probability that a team wins or covers the spread. Player availability, team strength, injuries, lineup changes, pace, opponent quality, exact travel itineraries, and other basketball variables are outside the present calculation. The factor definitions and weights can be tested and refined once historical game outcomes are incorporated.

Why relative comparison matters

A difficult schedule condition is not automatically a Schedule Loss. If both teams arrive under the same burden, the conditions may largely cancel. The model therefore emphasizes relative disadvantage within a matchup, then aggregates those differences to evaluate the overall schedule faced by each team.

Data architecture
